## Abstract
Educational institutions are increasingly significant consumers of electronic devices, yet school-level e-waste management is under-studied. This project asked: **How effective are Victoria, BC school institutions in implementing sustainable e-waste management?** I used an evaluation-based mixed-method approach centered on a survey of **6 school administrators** from **public and independent schools**, paired with document analysis across **10+** reports and files. I analyzed results using qualitative content analysis with a custom coding scheme and descriptive statistics. The results indicate low administrator awareness of e-waste categories, weak alignment between e-waste-specific policies and actions compared to general waste programs, and reliance on external e-waste handlers without visibility into recycling processes or constructive waste metrics. Overall, despite high technology integration, school e-waste management appears fragmented and reactive. I presented findings to local coordinators and administrators to support a framework initiative for management systems and education programs, and identified priorities for stronger measurement, training, and coordination.

## Context + Objective
This project began as an AP Research study on e-waste management in Victoria, BC school institutions. The core problem was not a lack of concern. It was inconsistent definitions, unclear ownership, limited monitoring, and a gap between technology adoption and sustainability practices.

**Objective:** turn messy real world inputs into measurable indicators and decision-ready reporting, then use the outputs to support local action and framework discussions.

## Approach
- Performed a literature review and synthesized three themes: policy and industry prioritizations, educator practice and EdTech growth versus environmental awareness, and limitations of student engagement in sustainability.
- Designed and administered an administrator survey measuring awareness, policies, actions, and self-evaluation practices related to e-waste.
- Collected and parsed **10+** reports and files across **6** institutions, then normalized formats for analysis.
- Built a pipeline that separates ingest, clean, analyze, and report stages so outputs are reproducible.
- Applied qualitative content analysis using a custom coding scheme, supported by descriptive statistics.
- Produced decision-ready tables and figures that make gaps visible, including where policies exist without measurement or ownership.
- Presented findings to coordinators and administrators to support a framework initiative and education program discussions.
- Protected respondent identity by aggregating results and removing identifying details.

## Results
- Analyzed **10+** reports and files across **6** institutions.
- Analyzed survey responses from **6** school administrators in public and independent schools.
- Found that administrator awareness of e-waste categories is generally low.
- Found that e-waste-specific policies and actions are underdeveloped and not strongly aligned compared to general waste programs.
- Found that administrators often rely on external e-waste handlers without visibility into recycling processes or access to constructive waste metrics.
- Characterized local school e-waste management as fragmented and reactive despite high technology integration.
- Presented findings to school-level coordinators and administrators and supported a framework initiative for management systems and education programs.
- Protected respondent identity by aggregating results and removing identifying details in reporting.

## Future Research
- Focus the evaluation on secondary schools with high rates of educational technology adoption, including departments responsible for electronic innovation, device programs, and technology growth.
- Map the lifecycle of devices from procurement to deployment to storage to disposal, then identify where ownership and measurement break down by department.
- Engineer a management system that increases participation and tracking, including standardized collection points, status measurements, and education modules for staff and students.
- Pilot the system in one or more schools and measure adoption and outcomes over time using longitudinal tracking, with clear indicators for participation, visibility, and diversion practices.
- Compare outcomes across departments to identify which structures predict stronger compliance and which departments consistently lack ownership or measurement.
